The first screen of any flow should have the fewest possible decisions. Every additional control on screen one is a tax paid by every user, including the ones who'll never touch it.

## Heuristics

- **Defaults beat options.** Pick a sensible default and tuck the override behind "Advanced" or a context menu.
- **Splits beat menus.** A two-button "Sign in / Create account" is faster to parse than a five-item menu.
- **Steps beat scrolling.** Long forms are easier to complete as 3 short pages than 1 long one — the brain treats each "Next" as a small win.

## What this isn't

Progressive disclosure ≠ hiding important stuff. The user must always know:

1. Where they are in the flow
2. What they have to do next
3. How to get back

If your "step 2 of 5" indicator is missing, you've made the flow shorter on screen and longer in feeling.
