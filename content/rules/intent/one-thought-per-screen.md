Every screen has a job. The job is one of these:

- Tell the user something
- Ask them something
- Let them do something

If your screen is doing two of those, the user has to spend cognitive effort figuring out which one to engage with first — and that's where drop-off lives.

## The one-sentence test

Before you ship a screen, write its purpose as a single sentence:

- ✅ *"Pick a date for the return flight."*
- ✅ *"Confirm the booking total before paying."*
- ❌ *"Show the user their trip and let them edit it and also upsell them airport parking."*

If the sentence has an "and" load-bearing in the middle, split the screen.

## Practical applications

- **Forms:** group fields by question they answer, not by data type.
- **Settings:** one panel per concern (Notifications, Privacy, Account) — never one mega-page.
- **Modals:** if your modal has tabs, it's probably a page.
