When everything is coloured, nothing stands out. Reserve colour for *meaning* (success, warning, danger, brand accent) and use type for *structure*.

## The greyscale test

Take a screenshot, desaturate it. Can you still tell:

- What the page is about (h1)
- The major sections (h2)
- The primary action (filled button vs. text link)
- What's secondary (muted body copy)

If yes — your hierarchy is robust. If you needed colour to read it, your structure is doing too little work.

## Practical scale

A four-step type ramp gets you very far:

| Role         | Size     | Weight |
|--------------|----------|--------|
| Page title   | 28–32px  | 700    |
| Section head | 18–20px  | 600    |
| Body         | 14–16px  | 400    |
| Caption      | 12–13px  | 400    |

Add weight (`600`, `700`) before you add a fifth size. Weight is cheaper to introduce than scale.

## What colour *is* for

- **State:** error red, success green, warning amber.
- **Brand moments:** the one accent colour, used sparingly on primary CTAs and highlights.
- **Decoration:** carefully — backgrounds, illustrations, never on type that conveys meaning.
